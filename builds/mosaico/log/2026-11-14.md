The Mosaico's own cell is 80 mAh, so I built it a power-bank case. It holds the
Mosaico with the camera and Interaction modules attached. Underneath sits a
2000 mAh 103450 LiPo (10 × 34 × 50 mm) and the charge/boost board from the 8x8
build. The board's 5 V output runs into the Mosaico's own USB-C port, so the
Mosaico itself stays unmodified.

The battery and board ride on a sled that slides into a dovetail groove in the
body. Two M3 self-tapping screws hold its end wall to the body. To load the
Mosaico, pull the sled, slide the device in from that end, and screw the sled
back. It's modelled in SolidCraft, with the core and Interaction Module rebuilt
from Espressif's published CAD.

What the test prints changed:

- **Camera end:** 5 mm too long. I had measured the camera module with its hinge.
- **Retention:** lips over the core, plus a low stop bar at the camera end. The camera can still flip out.
- **USB-C opening:** tightened, with a 12 mm cable slot below it.
- **Key access:** the side openings grew 2 mm toward the Interaction Module.
- **Length:** the real Interaction Module pushed the sled 2 mm proud. The body and sled both gained 2 mm.

The battery started as a 1000 mAh 102050 and became a 2000 mAh 103450 before
the last print. The body's bay already fit it, so only the sled's rails moved.

> **Note:** The board's power-bank IC cuts its 5 V output after 30 s under about
> 80 mA, per the datasheet. I'm keeping it until that bites. A TP4056 plus MT3608
> is the fallback.
