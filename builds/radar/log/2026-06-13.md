Started from a modern-SDK fork of a plane-radar project on the ESP32-C3, then more or less gutted it. The stock build locked up periodically — it was firing a burst of ADS-B requests and buffering whole response bodies. Fixed that with a single request on a backoff and a **stream-parse** so I only ever pull the fields I need. That alone stopped the lockups.

From there it was a founding rebuild in one long session: a **dual-core split** with the network fetch on its own task so rendering never stalls, and **smooth plane interpolation** so targets glide between the 3-second polls instead of teleporting. Added an **ESP32-S3 Super Mini target** for the better radio, plus WiFi retry hardening so a flaky connect recovers instead of hanging. Then the bells and whistles: **16-bit color at 80 MHz SPI** on the S3, and the **radar sweep with a comet trail paced at 30 fps**. By the end it was a live scope — round LCD, green grid, real callsigns — sitting in the printed slatted cube on the desk.
