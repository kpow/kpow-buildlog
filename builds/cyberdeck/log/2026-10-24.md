I kept using the half-dead original while the replacement was on its way. It ran off the UART port, with a 5V supply on the breadboard for the LEDs. Then I seated the TPS protoboard one pin off in its header. The TPS input and output went straight back down a GPIO, and that finished the original board.

The spare had already arrived, so it became the main board. Good thing the old one was the one taking the risks.

Bring-up on the new board took a couple of days, and everything on the bench works. The 5V bus runs off a BTF-Lighting 5V 3A supply instead of the DP100 I'd planned on.

The TPS boards aren't back in yet. Quick metering says they're probably fine, but they still need a proper test.

> **Next:** put a 2N7000 gate between the GPIO and the TPS board, so a misplaced header can't reach the MCU again.
