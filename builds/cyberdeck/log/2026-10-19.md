I wired an Adafruit TPS61169 boost board in with VIN and GND swapped while adding it to drive the noodles. There was a puff of smoke and everything went dark. It sat on a protoboard with two more TPS boards, all set to 5V.

The board looked completely dead, with no serial port at all. Most of it wasn't:

- **CH340 driver:** the macOS driver extension was disabled, so the UART port enumerated with no `/dev/cu.*` node. Enabling it brought the port back.
- **5V path:** dead. Fault current came from native USB's VBUS, through the board's 5V rail and out the 5V pin into the reversed TPS. Native USB went with it.
- **Onboard WS2812:** its GPIO stopped driving it.
- **Orange matrix:** a brownout, not damage. 16 WS2812Bs pull ~960mA, fed through the board off a 500mA USB port.

The SoC, 16MB flash and 8MB PSRAM all checked out. A `uart-serial` build sent `Serial` to UART0, and the firmware ran at 38 fps over the CH340. Ordered a replacement anyway.

> **Lesson:** the dev board's 5V pin is a convenience tap, not a power rail. Every load fault on it ran through the MCU.
